The discovery

Semaglutide, not lenalidomide, is now the swing factor, and it is fragile

Dr. Reddy's Laboratories has already stopped being a North America-led business — North America fell from a 44.9% peak (FY25) to 27.4% of revenue in Q1FY27, below even its FY21 share of 37.8%, while the rest of the business grew 23.5% in FY26 — which makes semaglutide, not lenalidomide, the swing factor, and semaglutide has slipped twice in 8 months, ending in a ₹240 cr write-down (54% of Q1FY27 net profit) and a ~46% cut to the FY27 plan (12 → 6-7 mn pens).

Why it matters

  • The lenalidomide cliff is already in the numbers: at 27.4% of revenue, North America is below its FY21 level, so the debate should move from 'how big is the cliff' to 'what replaces it'.
  • Growth outside North America is broad (Europe +55%, India +16%, Emerging Markets +23%, PSAI +3%, FY26). Part of it was acquired (Agrees to buy Haleon's non-US NRT brands (Nicotinell) for GBP 500m).
  • Semaglutide is the largest new organic lever, and its setbacks came from different failure points: 30 Oct 2025 (Health Canada): Health Canada notice of non-compliance on semaglutide submission; 9 Jul 2026 (company): Reg-30 filing: semaglutide batches out of specification (API issue).
  • Its supply chain runs through an external partner (OneSource Specialty Pharma, finished-dose (pen) contract manufacturer; API in-house): a dependency that shows up in partner and press disclosures, not in the segment table.
  • Sensitivity: the Q1FY27 provision (₹240 cr) equals 54% of that quarter's net profit; the plan cut removes about 5.5 mn pens of supply (midpoint).
