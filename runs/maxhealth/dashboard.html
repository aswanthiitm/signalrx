The discovery

A government payor rule, not demand, is behind the oncology drag

Max Healthcare Institute's ARPOB stall (+2.6% in Q3FY26, +0.0% in Q4FY26) was a payor-policy shock, not weak demand: after the CGHS drug-billing order (Dec 2024) and CGHS package-rate revision (13 Oct 2025), it stopped administering high-value patented chemotherapy to institutional (CGHS) patients from Nov 2025, cutting oncology from 26% to 22% of inpatient revenue — so reported growth (16%) understates the rest of the business (20%), and the Q1FY27 ARPOB rebound (+5%) owes about 1.2pp to the CGHS tariff revision.

Why it matters

  • The drag is policy-made, so it reverses on policy, not on demand: management calls it structural unless the payor's terms improve.
  • Headline growth understates core growth by 4pp. Cross-check: the share shift alone implies oncology revenue growth of -1.8% and non-oncology growth of 22.3%, close to the reported 20%.
  • Read the 'normalisation' guidance carefully: it falls 13 months after the change, i.e. when the base effect laps. Year-on-year growth normalises, but the lost oncology revenue does not come back unless terms change.
  • ARPOB optics: of the +5% in Q1FY27, about 1.2pp is the tariff revision (~₹140 cr a year), leaving core ARPOB growth near 3.8%.
  • Company-specific: peers grew revenue per patient faster in the same quarter (Apollo Hospitals +8% vs +5%). The exposure comes from the payor relationship on record: CGHS (large CGHS / institutional book; high-value chemo for institutional patients discontinued).

Strongest counterargument

Management has a dated fix: 'Management expects oncology growth to normalise from Q3 FY27, fully by Q4 FY27' (Dec 2026). If it lands on time, the financial drag fades; this becomes a falsification test. Also: The lost revenue is described as low-margin, so the earnings damage is smaller than the revenue damage. New-capacity costs are a second, smaller drag on margins cited in the same note. Management's position: "Impact is structural unless CGHS terms improve; in discussion with government on below-cost supply." "Oncology growth to normalise from Q3 FY27, fully by Q4 FY27."

Secondary discovery

Announced capacity is running well ahead of commissioned capacity Moderate

Max Healthcare Institute's bed plan is running far ahead of its builds: operational beds rose 630 in the year to Q1FY27 (to 5,379), but the 9,000–9,500 target for FY28 needs 2,066–2,351 a year, 3.3–3.7x the current pace, and the target has been restated 3 times.

Why it matters

  • Hospital earnings follow commissioned beds, not announced ones; consensus built on the headline target overstates the near-term run-rate.
  • The gap leaves 21 months to add 3,621–4,121 beds.
  • Dated delays (clearances) are on record, so part of the gap is execution, not just phasing.
  • New beds start at low occupancy and carry pre-commissioning costs, so an accelerated build would dilute margins first.

Counterargument

No alternative explanation weakened the finding.

Falsification

  1. Operational beds reach 6,928 within 9 months (the run-rate the low target requires).
  2. Management formally phases the target out beyond the stated year (which resolves the gap by moving the goalpost).
