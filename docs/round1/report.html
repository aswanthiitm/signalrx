Idea. In pharma and healthcare the important story usually sits between documents, not inside one: an FDA inspection at a contract plant the company doesn't own, a government payor's billing order, a loss-of-exclusivity date, a partner's filing, a peer's results for the same quarter. SignalRx builds a dated evidence ledger across these independent source families, lets the numbers say where something moved, sends an LLM investigator to find why, and then tries to kill its own hypothesis before reporting anything. A summariser reads one source; SignalRx reports only findings that need at least two independent families and calculations no single source states.

Workflow: company → plan → evidence → discovery

resolve→harvest→verify inputs→profile→scan→plan→investigate→expand 3rd parties→hypothesise→skeptic→adversarial search→score→write→verify output→render

Blue = deterministic code; orange = LLM. The LLM proposes; code disposes (it computes, checks and scores).

  1. Harvest: 5–10 years of statements and quarterly results, Reg-30 filings, FDA recalls/approvals, dated headlines.
  2. Scan: where the numbers moved: mix shifts, segment step-downs, reported-vs-adjusted growth gaps, ARPOB stalls, announced-vs-commissioned capacity.
  3. Plan: ranks the Track D angle library (D1 model drift, D2 regulatory-before-coverage, D3 geography, plus third-party plants, payor policy, capacity, narrative gap) by archetype fit × anomalies, with one open LLM-proposed slot.
  4. Investigate: a Claude tool loop per angle (search, fetch, PDF search, FDA/NSE/news). Each record needs a verbatim quote from a fetched document with exactly matching numbers, or it is rejected.
  5. Expand: each third party found (contract maker, supplier, payor) gets its own regulator probe. This is how the Cipla finding is reached.
  6. Hypothesise → challenge → score → write, then the verifier re-traces every number in the prose.

Generalises across the track, with no company tuning

  • Universe from NSE index files at run time (today Sun, Cipla, Dr. Reddy's, Apollo, Max): reshuffles need no code.
  • Archetype from the company's own text and segments (formulations / API-CDMO / hospital / diagnostics) routes to the right KPIs. The pilots cover two archetypes on one code path, which the tie-break pool (Divi's, Lupin, Fortis, Syngene…) shares.
  • Sector knowledge pack, not company knowledge: FDA/Health Canada/EU escalation ladders, a CGHS/NPPA/Sec-232/BIOSECURE watchlist, an LOE calendar and a controlled metric vocabulary. A test fails the build if any company name appears in code.

Triangulation and contradiction detection

  • Source families (company · each regulator · each counterparty · each publisher): a discovery needs ≥2, and the strength grade counts them.
  • Recompute, don't trust: reported growth is recomputed from filed levels. In the pilot, one outlet printed Dr. Reddy's Q1 at +6% and NA at +35%; the filings give −5.6% and −35.4%, so both were dropped and logged.
  • Hierarchy: regulator = filing > transcript/counterparty > broker/press > aggregator > blog. Overruled figures go to an audit log; derived values carry tolerance bands. Peers are compared for the same quarter.

Self-falsification and temporal reasoning

The skeptic is code that re-runs the numbers for each alternative: sector-wide · acquired · one-off (adds it back) · temporary (dated fix becomes a falsifier) · second driver with timing · breadth · low-margin · already-in-headlines · data conflicts. Live runs also send an adversarial investigator after the leading hypothesis. Each event carries a date and a precision; the agent measures regulator → disclosure → headline → results lags. From the pilots:

  • Cipla: the lenalidomide LOE (31-Jan-26) lands after the step quarter, so the finding was resized to "~51% explained".
  • Dr. Reddy's: broad ex-NA growth reframed "concentrated replacement" into "semaglutide is the swing factor".
  • Max: "normalisation by Q3–Q4 FY27" falls 13 months after the change, which the agent flags as base-effect lapping, not recovery.

Output: interactive dashboard plus PDF research note (same content)

Top to bottom: the discovery (one sentence, citation chips), key-number tiles, a chart with dated event markers, why it matters, the strongest counterargument, falsification conditions, a timeline, evidence strength with reasons, the skeptic table, the full evidence trail (stance, family, passage, link; filterable), every calculation with formula and inputs, the hypothesis board including rejected ideas, the audit log and the agent trace. Every claim is one click from its passage and URL. The PDF is the same page printed to A4.

Evidence strength rubric (shown with its reasons)

  • Strong: ≥4–6 independent families incl. a primary filing, most skeptic tests survived, no refutation, all conflicts resolved.
  • Moderate: ≥3 families incl. a primary filing, but key attribution rests on management commentary or the press.
  • Weak: thin or single-family support, or a refuting test. A weak finding is still shown, with that label.

The ranking score is 0.35 novelty + 0.30 evidence + 0.20 materiality + 0.15 robustness. Novelty rewards non-company families, a third-party record that leads the company's disclosure, and derived calculations; it is penalised when headlines already carry the core terms.
